ADC / FPGA feature-access audit — 2026-09-30

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
Read-only review of the AD7606C-16BSTZ (carrier U1) connected through J2 to
the iCE40UP5K-SG48 (module U2). This checks the present schematic, PCB
copper, FPGA constraints and the manufacturers' device documentation.

The existing four-lane, software-controlled serial interface is correctly
connected end to end. It can access the ADC register map and its major
software features. It does not expose every interface feature: DOUTE-H
are unconnected, and FRSTDATA reaches only a test point. These five signals
can be added using already available FPGA GPIO on the mating connector.

Four lanes cannot carry all eight channels at 1 MSPS with both status and
CRC enabled. The current 200 kSPS target has sufficient transfer bandwidth.
Eight lanes resolve this bandwidth bottleneck, but maximum-rate operation
also requires a clock and FPGA timing implementation that have not yet
been demonstrated. This review made no schematic, PCB or pin-map changes.

Missing signals and available expansion
--------------------------------------
U1.15 FRSTDATA (ADC_FIRST) connects to TP1, with no route to the FPGA.
It identifies the first conversion word and can provide an independent
framing check. CS and an exact clock count permit operation without it;
the optional status channel ID provides another check with extra clocks.

U1.19, .20, .21 and .22 are DOUTE, DOUTF, DOUTG and DOUTH. All four are
intentionally unconnected. Eight-lane operation is consequently unavailable.

Recommendation: reserve five of these ten contacts for FRSTDATA and DOUTE-H,
then update the schematic, PCB, canonical pin map and PCF together. Choose
the assignment from physical routing and timing constraints. No new FPGA
device or module connector is needed. J2.12 is also a global-clock-capable
input and could be retained for a future external clock. J2.25 and .30 are
marked unused on the carrier but do reach GPIO on the module.

Mode, supplies and reset
-----------------------
OS0/1/2 (U1.3/4/5) are high: software mode. PAR/SER (6) is high: serial.
STBY_N (7) is high and RANGE (8) is low; both are correctly fixed for
software mode. WR_N (10) is high. The unused parallel DB0-2 (16-18) and
DB12-15 (30-33) pins are grounded as specified for serial operation.

ADC VDRIVE is nominal 3.3 V; all used FPGA I/O banks are nominal 3.3 V.
These are separate regulators on the two boards, fed from common VIN5V.
Their nominal LVCMOS input/output limits are compatible; no ADC digital
signal applies its 5 V analog supply to an FPGA input. Rail startup order,
tolerances and actual waveforms remain hardware validation items.

All ADC grounds, AVCC pins, mode straps, reference/regulator capacitors,
and ten local decoupling capacitors were checked for connectivity. All
seven J2 ground contacts connect on both boards. REGCAP_A and REGCAP_D
remain separate. REFSEL is high, selecting the internal reference.

10 kohm pulls hold CONVST low (R21), CS_N high (R22), and RESET high (R23).
After the rails settle, FPGA startup must issue a defined full reset and
configure the ADC before sampling. Full reset requires at least 3.2 us
high, followed by at least 274 us setup time. Rev. A Table 3 footnote 3
additionally requires the first power-up wait plus device setup time to
exceed 2 seconds; the 10 ms power-up minimum alone is insufficient.
A conservative sequence waits 2.1 s after stable rails, then produces a
fresh >=4 us high reset pulse and waits >=274 us after its falling edge.
Do not assume the power-on pull-up alone proves a valid reset sequence.

CONFIG 0x02[4:3] is 10 for four lanes and 11 for eight; preserve other bits
when changing it. CRC enable is in 0x21[2]; preserve the other diagnostic
enable bits, including the reset-enabled ROM check. Diagnostics and power
modes require a deliberate configuration/restore sequence, not just wires.

The analog front ends currently target 0-5 V on CH1-4 (AUX1, AUX2, AUX3,
AUX4) and CH6/8 (CT envelopes), and +/-2.5 V on CH5/7 (CT raw signals).
Changing ADC range registers does not change those front ends or expose
the full differential input capability. Other ranges/reference choices
would require a separate analog design review.

Bandwidth and timing limits
---------------------------
Clocks per complete eight-channel sample set:
Lanes      Raw       With status       With CRC       With status + CRC
4           32            48              48                  64
8           16            24              32                  40

At VDRIVE >=3.1 V, the ADC permits at most 63.5 MHz SCLK. Therefore a
four-lane status+CRC frame takes at least 64/63.5 MHz = 1.008 us, already
longer than a 1 MSPS sample interval before CS or other overhead. This is
a hard interface bandwidth limitation, even with pipelined conversion.
Eight lanes reduce that frame to 40 clocks (0.630 us at 63.5 MHz).

At the documented provisional 25 MHz SCLK and 200 kSPS target, four-lane
status+CRC transfer takes 2.56 us. With 0.65 us maximum conversion time,
the simple no-oversampling schedule totals 3.21 us within the 5 us period,
leaving margin for framing/control. This is a timing budget, not measured
or synthesized performance. Oversampling and phase delays need new budgets.

For 1 MSPS, reads must overlap conversions. Data must be read before the
next BUSY falling edge, with a 25 ns guard. Using the full 0.50-0.65 us
conversion-time range conservatively leaves 0.825 us between worst-case
updates after that guard. Forty clocks at 48 MHz take 0.833 us even before
control/FPGA delays, so nominal 48 MHz is not a robust full-rate guarantee.

The FPGA's internal HFOSC is nominal 48 MHz, with +/-20% industrial
temperature frequency tolerance (38.4-57.6 MHz) and 40-60% duty-cycle
limits. No dedicated FPGA reference oscillator was found; the module's
40 MHz crystal belongs to the ESP32. A full-rate claim needs a defined
clock source, all-corners timing closure and a conversion/read schedule.
The simple clock-count arithmetic does not by itself select that clock.

The ADC changes subsequent DOUT bits after rising SCLK edges; maximum
access delay is 15.7 ns at VDRIVE >=3.1 V, minimum hold is 7 ns. SDI is
captured on falling SCLK edges with 9 ns setup. A naive opposite-edge
DOUT sample at 48 MHz has only 10.42 ns and is not sufficient. Constrain
the complete FPGA-output/board/ADC/board/FPGA-input path and use an
appropriate capture phase. Also account for the first-bit CS enable time
(18 ns max), framing and the 25 ns write/read interval above 50 MHz.

No Tesla acquisition HDL or timing constraints were found, only the PCF
pin constraints. Consequently this audit does not prove FPGA timing,
resource fit, CRC implementation, startup behavior or measured signal
integrity. Bring-up should exercise register CRC, all eight distinct test
patterns, channel IDs, reset/error flags and BUSY timeout handling.

Verification evidence and limits
--------------------------------
324 automated checks passed with zero failures. They cover all 64 ADC
pins, ten digital paths, ten spare GPIO routes, pulls/decoupling, package
pin roles, schematic/PCB agreement and PCF correspondence. Physical
continuity was evaluated using KiCad's native connectivity, not net names
alone. These are design-file checks, not measurements on assembled boards.

Fresh KiCad 10.0.6 DRC: carrier 0 violations, 0 schematic parity issues,
2 existing USB_D+/USB_D- opens unrelated to the ADC; module 0 violations
and 0 opens. Standard project-ignored checks are recorded in the JSON.
The secondary kct checker reports module GND and RF_ANT incompleteness;
native KiCad DRC does not reproduce those opens, and the ADC-relevant
ground contacts pass native connectivity checks. This discrepancy is
recorded rather than treated as a missing ADC ground.

The adjacent adc-fpga-feature-audit.json contains per-pin connectivity,
test results, source SHA-256 hashes, pin mappings and DRC results. The
source designs and PCF hashes were rechecked after producing this report.
Use the current PCF/netlists as the mapping authority over older routing
notes. No connectivity changes are included in this audit.
